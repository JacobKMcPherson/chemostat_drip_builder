Checkerboard Titration Builder

Design the recommended six-block 8×8 checkerboard: seven nonzero concentrations of X crossed with seven nonzero concentrations of Y, plus a zero control on each axis, repeated across six blocks of a 384-well plate at 40 µL/well — either fixed Z (six technical replicates at one Z level) or variable Z (a different Z level per block), or a minimal 30 µL X+Y+Z-only well. Set each factor's top concentration, unit (µg/mL or µM, interconverted by molecular weight), and starting stock, and the page computes the working-stock dilution from that stock, the 2-fold or ~3.167-fold (half-log) strip dilution recipe, and every pipetting step — then exports it all as a FAIR data package.

1 · Design & recipe

Fixed at a 384-well plate, six 8×8 blocks (rows A–H / I–P × columns 1–8 / 9–16 / 17–24). Pick the experiment scheme and the strip dilution recipe.

2 · Factor concentrations

Top (block-1, Cmax) final concentration, unit, and optional molecular weight for each factor. Switching the unit auto-converts the top and source-stock concentrations using the molecular weight.

3 · Working stock preparation

How much of each factor's starting (source) stock solution and diluent to combine to make the 4×/3×/2× working solution the strips (or reservoir) are filled from.

4 · Strip dilution recipe & concentration ladders

Per-tube volumes for the chosen recipe, and the resulting concentration series for X, Y, and Z.

6 · Blocks

One row per block. Z assignment and an optional sample label/notes per block.

7 · Generated prep steps

Ordered bench steps: strip fills, serial dilutions, and plate additions, with volumes and the recommended pipette.

8 · Well-by-well data entry

Enter a recorded value and notes per well. Feeds the FAIR export's sample_values.csv.

9 · Dataset metadata & FAIR export

Everything above, plus this metadata, is packaged as a Frictionless tabular data package.


      

    

  


  

    

      
10 · Bench procedure

      
A general run order; the exact volumes and pipettes are generated in sections 3, 4 and 7.

    

    

      
    
        
  1. Set the scheme and recipe in section 1, the per-factor concentrations/units/MW in section 2, and the starting stocks/overage in section 3.
    2. 
        
  2. Prepare the X1–X6 and Y1–Y6 strips (and Z1–Z6 if Z is variable) with the tube volumes in section 4, arranged side by side in a 96-well-format strip holder so a 12-channel P100 can serially dilute all twelve (or twenty) tubes together, mixing before each transfer and discarding one transfer volume from the last diluted position. Leave the final position (tube 8, or Z6) untouched as the zero/vehicle control.
    3. 
        
  3. Add X, then Y with an adjustable 8-channel P20 at 96-well spacing (aspirate) then 384-well spacing (dispense): X across each block's columns 1–8, Y down each block's rows 1–8, highest concentration nearest the block's top-left corner.
    4. 
        
  4. Add Z and/or the common assay mixture per section 7: fixed Z uses a single 2×Z/assay reservoir mix added 20 µL/well with a 16-channel P100; variable Z adds 10 µL/well from each block's Z strip, then 10 µL/well common mixture; the minimal scheme stops after X/Y/Z with no further addition.
    5. 
        
  5. Replace tips once they have touched well liquid before returning to any source strip or reservoir, especially while dispensing Y across wells that already contain X.
    6. 
        
  6. Record values as you read them in section 8 — the plate view and export update immediately.
